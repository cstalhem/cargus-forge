# Clearances

Free floor space around furniture, in centimetres. Swedish building rules point to SS 91 42 21:2006 for room dimensions, but that standard is sold by SIS and is not online, so this file gives the figures that Boverket quotes from it and fills the gaps with the UK accessible-housing standard and Irish universal-design guidance. Both set minimums for new homes. In an existing home, treat them as measures of what is comfortable and usable.

## Beds and bedrooms
- [standard] For a bed that someone with a disability may need help at, leave 60 cm free along the far long side for a helper. This is the SS 91 42 21 normal-level figure that Boverket quotes in its BBR guidance. [1]
- [standard] On the near long side, leave 120 × 120 cm free in front of the bed, so a wheelchair user can reach it (Boverket's BBR guidance on accessible bed spaces). [1]
- [standard] Principal double bedroom: a clear zone at least 75 cm wide on both sides and at the foot of the bed (UK Approved Document M, category M4(2)). Other double bedrooms: 75 cm on one side and at the foot. Single and twin beds: 75 cm on one side of each bed. [2]
- [standard] Every bedroom: a clear route at least 75 cm wide from the door to the window (UK Approved Document M, category M4(2)). [2]
- [convention] Irish universal-design guidance asks for 80 cm clear on both sides and at the foot of a double bed, and 80 cm beside a single bed. [3]
- [convention] Plan 60 to 80 cm beside each side of a double bed that someone gets in from, and at least 75 cm at the foot where people walk past. 60 cm works but feels tight; 75 to 80 cm is what the accessibility rules and guidance above ask for. [1] [2] [3]
- [convention] A single bed can stand with one long side against the wall, since the standards ask for free space on one side only. [2]
- [convention] A double bed that two people share works best with both long sides free, so neither has to climb over the other.

## Seating and living room
- [convention] Leave 30 to 45 cm between the sofa and the coffee table: close enough to reach a cup, far enough to pass your legs. [4]
- [convention] Where people walk between furniture to reach another room or a window, keep a clear route at least 75 cm wide; 80 cm in the Irish "UD Home Plus" level. [3]
- [convention] For a comfortable main route through a room, aim for 90 to 120 cm. Irish universal-design guidance recommends this width between items, in front of windows and between doors. [3]
- [standard] A living room should leave a clear turning space of 150 × 150 cm, or an ellipse of 140 × 170 cm, for a wheelchair (UK Approved Document M, furniture schedule). [2]
- [standard] Inside a Swedish dwelling, Boverket uses a 130 cm turning circle for an indoor wheelchair when judging accessibility. [1]

## Dining
- [convention] Leave 120 cm clear on at least two adjoining sides of a dining table, and on all sides at the higher "UD Home Plus" level (Irish universal-design guidance). [3]
- [convention] From the table edge to a wall, allow 81 cm when nobody passes behind the seated diners, 91 cm for someone to edge past, and 112 cm for someone to walk past (NKBA Kitchen Planning Guidelines). [5]
- [convention] Give each diner about 60 cm of table edge: NKBA asks for a 61 cm wide knee space per seated diner. [5]
- [convention] Leave at least 80 cm between the table edge and the wall, so chairs can be pulled out. [6]

## Kitchen
- [standard] Keep at least 120 cm clear in front of and between all kitchen units and appliances (UK Approved Document M, category M4(2)). [2]
- [convention] Between facing worktops, leave 120 to 150 cm; 150 to 180 cm at the higher "UD Home Plus" level (Irish universal-design guidance). [3]
- [convention] A work aisle should be at least 107 cm wide for one cook and 122 cm for several; a walkway at least 91 cm (NKBA Kitchen Planning Guidelines). [5]

## Doors and getting furniture in
- [standard] Inside a Swedish dwelling, doors should give a free passage of at least 76 cm, and the entrance door 80 cm (Boverket's BBR guidance). Measure the free passage, not the door leaf, before you buy a large piece. [1]
- [convention] Keep a door's full swing clear of furniture, so the door opens all the way.

## Safety
- [convention] Anchor bookcases, chests of drawers, wardrobes and TVs to the wall, because heavy furniture that tips can seriously injure a child (Konsumentverket's child-safety advice). [7]
- [convention] Choose wall fixings to suit the wall material, and keep heavy things in the lowest drawers and shelves.

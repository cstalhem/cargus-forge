# Room layouts

How to arrange furniture by room type. The clearances these layouts must keep are in `clearances.md`, and the furniture sizes in `sizing.md`.

## Any room
- [convention] Plan on paper first: draw the room to scale with doors, door swings, windows, radiators and sockets, then place the largest piece before the smaller ones.
- [convention] Mark a large piece's footprint on the floor with masking tape before you buy it, to check that it fits and leaves room to walk.
- [standard] In every bedroom, leave a clear route at least 75 cm wide from the door to the window (UK Approved Document M, category M4(2)). [2]
- [convention] Keep the same 75 cm clear route between furniture and in front of windows in the other rooms too, as Irish universal-design guidance asks. [3]
- [convention] Keep the main walking route along one side of a seating or dining group, not through its middle.

## Living room
- [convention] Group the seating around one focal point, such as a window, a fireplace or the TV, with the coffee table 30 to 45 cm from the sofa. [1]
- [convention] In a living room that people also pass through, a sofa set out from the wall with a walkway behind it keeps the route out of the seating group.
- [taste] Sofa facing two armchairs across a coffee table; an L-shaped corner sofa that leaves the rest of the floor open; two sofas facing each other for conversation; a sofa back to the dining area to divide an open-plan room.

## Bedroom
- [standard] At least one double bedroom should allow free space on both long sides and at the foot of the bed, and every other bedroom on one side (UK Approved Document M, category M4(2)). [2]
- [standard] Swedish homes larger than 55 m² should have room for a double bed in at least one bedroom or separable part of a room (Boverket's guidance on BBR 3:222). [4]
- [convention] Put the head of the bed against a wall and keep both long sides free, so both sleepers can get in without climbing over each other.
- [convention] Leave the route from the door to the window and to the wardrobe free, so you can open the wardrobe doors fully.
- [taste] Bed centred on the wall facing the door; bed against the wall beside the window to keep the window wall free; a headboard wall with built-in storage on both sides.

## Dining area
- [convention] Put the dining table in or close to the kitchen, so food and dishes are not carried through doors. If the table is in the living room, keep a small table in the kitchen for quick meals. [3]
- [convention] Leave 120 cm clear on at least two adjoining sides of the table (Irish universal-design guidance). [3]
- [taste] A round table, which seats one more person in a small room and has no corners to walk around; a rectangular table along a wall or a window; an extendable table that stays small day to day.

## Kitchen
- [convention] Place the sink next to or opposite the hob and the fridge. Keep the sum of the three walking distances between hob, sink and fridge at most 790 cm, with no single leg shorter than 120 cm or longer than 270 cm (NKBA Kitchen Planning Guidelines). [5]
- [convention] No leg of that triangle should cut through an island or another obstacle by more than 30 cm. [5]
- [convention] No door should block the use of an appliance, and appliance doors should not hit each other. [5]
- [taste] A galley kitchen with two facing rows; an L-shaped kitchen with a dining table in the open corner; a kitchen island that also serves as a breakfast bar.

## Small homes and studios
- [standard] In a Swedish home of at most 35 m², living and sleeping may share one space, and a bed that also works as a sofa is enough (Boverket's guidance on BBR 3:224). [4]
- [standard] In a home of 35 to 55 m², either the sleeping area or the kitchen must be possible to separate off as its own room (Boverket's guidance on BBR 3:223). [4]
- [taste] A sofa bed or daybed; a bed in an alcove behind a curtain or a shelf unit; a loft bed with a desk or sofa underneath; a folding wall bed.

## Home office
- [convention] A desk top of 75 × 120 to 150 cm at a working height of about 75 cm suits most work. [6]
- [convention] Place the desk away from noisy areas. [3]
- [taste] Desk facing the window for the view; desk side-on to the window to cut screen glare; a desk in a bedroom corner screened by a shelf.

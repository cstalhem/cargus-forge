# Sizing

How big furniture, rugs and art should be, in centimetres. Sizes in inches from the sources are converted and rounded to the nearest centimetre.

## Beds
- [convention] Swedish single mattresses are normally 90 × 200 cm, and also come as 80, 105 and 120 × 200 cm. Double mattresses are normally 160 or 180 × 200 cm, and also come as 140 × 200 cm. [1]
- [convention] Choose a bed long enough that your feet do not stick out past the end. [1]
- [convention] A double bed of 140 cm suits a smaller room; 160 to 180 cm gives more comfort. [10]
- [standard] When a plan has to show that a bedroom works, the UK furniture schedule uses a 150 × 200 cm principal double bed, a 135 × 190 cm other double, 90 × 190 cm single beds and 40 × 40 cm bedside tables (UK Approved Document M, Appendix D). Swedish beds are 200 cm long, so add 10 cm to its single and second-double lengths. [2] [1]

## Sofas, chairs and coffee tables
- [standard] For room planning, the UK furniture schedule uses an armchair of 85 × 85 cm, a two-seat sofa of 85 × 130 cm, a three-seat sofa of 85 × 185 cm and a coffee table of 50 × 105 cm, or 75 cm round (UK Approved Document M, Appendix D). [2]
- [convention] A coffee table about two thirds the length of the sofa looks in proportion; for a sofa with a chaise, measure only the main section. [3]
- [convention] A coffee table is usually about 45 cm high: level with the sofa seat or a little higher. [3]

## Dining tables
- [standard] For room planning, the UK furniture schedule uses an 80 cm wide dining table, 80 cm long for 2 people, 100 cm for 3, 120 cm for 4, 135 cm for 5, 150 cm for 6 and 165 cm for 7 (UK Approved Document M, Appendix D). [2]
- [convention] Allow about 60 cm of table edge per person. [4] [5]
- [convention] A round table for 4 is about 110 cm across, and one for 8 about 150 cm. A rectangular table for 4 is 70 to 90 cm wide and 120 to 150 cm long. [5]
- [convention] A dining table is 72 to 75 cm high, with at least 63 cm free under the top for the knees, and no more than 30 cm between the chair seat and the table top. [5]

## Storage and desks
- [standard] For room planning, the UK furniture schedule uses a double wardrobe of 60 × 120 cm, a chest of drawers of 45 × 75 cm and a desk with chair of 50 × 105 cm (UK Approved Document M, Appendix D). [2]
- [convention] A comfortable desk top is 75 cm deep and 120 to 150 cm wide, at a working height of about 75 cm. [5]

## Rugs
- [convention] Under a sofa group, let at least the front legs of the sofa and chairs stand on the rug; for a more unified group, put all the legs on it. [6]
- [convention] Choose a rug longer than the sofa, with about a third of the sofa standing on it. [7]
- [convention] Under a dining table, the rug should reach 60 to 80 cm past each long side of the table, so the chairs stay on it when pulled out, and it should be longer than the table. [7]
- [convention] Under a bed, let the rug reach 60 to 90 cm out on each side, so your feet land on it when you get up. [6]
- [convention] In a hall, choose a runner that leaves 10 to 15 cm of floor visible on each side. [6]
- [taste] One large rug that holds the whole seating group; a smaller rug under the coffee table only; two rugs to mark two zones in an open-plan room.

## Art
- [convention] Hang art so its centre is 145 to 152 cm above the floor. [8] [9]
- [convention] Above a sofa, make the art, or the whole group of frames, about two thirds to three quarters of the sofa's width. [9]
- [convention] Above a sofa, leave a gap between the bottom of the frame and the top of the sofa back; sources give 8 to 15 cm and 20 to 25 cm, so pick a gap in that range that keeps the art near eye height. [8] [9]
- [convention] In a gallery wall, keep 5 to 8 cm between frames and the same gap throughout; gaps of 15 cm or more make the frames read as separate pieces. [9] [8]
- [taste] A single large piece; a symmetrical grid of equal frames; an informal salon hang of mixed sizes; a picture ledge for art you change often.

# Outdoor furniture and materials

## Strength and safety
- [standard] Outdoor seating for home use is tested for strength, durability and stability under SS-EN 581-2:2026, based on users weighing up to 110 kg. The standard does not test resistance to ageing from light, temperature and moisture, so weather resistance is a separate question [1].
- [standard] SS-EN 581-1:2017 sets the general safety requirements for outdoor seating and tables, including shear and squeeze points where fingers can be caught [2].

## Wood
- [standard] Pressure-treated pine in class NTR A is for use class 4 under SS-EN 335: in contact with the ground or under high moisture load [3].
- [standard] Pressure-treated pine in class NTR AB is for use class 3.2 under SS-EN 335: above ground and unprotected from rain and moisture [4].
- [convention] Pressure-treated wood may be reused but never burned in a private stove or fire; take the waste to a recycling centre [3].
- [convention] Wear a P2 dust mask when sawing or sanding treated wood without good ventilation [3].
- [taste] Hardwood such as teak left to weather silver-grey or oiled to keep its colour; painted or stained pine; powder-coated aluminium or steel; woven polyrattan; cast concrete or stone.

## Balconies
- [standard] Where young children can be, 0.8 m of a balcony railing's height must be designed to resist climbing, and fixed climbable items next to the railing, such as planter boxes, are counted (BFS 2024:9) [5].
- [convention] Where young children use the balcony, keep movable furniture, planters and stacked pots that a child could climb away from the railing, for the same reason.
- [convention] Ask the landlord or housing association before placing heavy items on a balcony, such as large filled planters or a hot tub. How much load a balcony takes is a structural question for a professional, not something to estimate.

## Grilling
- [convention] The landlord or housing association decides whether grilling is allowed on a balcony [6].
- [convention] Stand the grill on a flat, preferably non-combustible base at least 1 m from a combustible wall, never leave it unattended, and keep water or a fire extinguisher at hand [6].
- [convention] Before each season, bend the hose between a gas bottle and the grill; replace it if cracks appear. Do not store gas bottles in a basement or attic [6].
- [convention] Never take a charcoal grill indoors, because coals that are not fully out give off carbon monoxide; put used charcoal in a non-combustible container, since it can glow for up to three days [6].

## Space
- [convention] Plan clearances around an outdoor dining table or seating group as for indoor furniture; the `furniture-layout` skill has the measures.

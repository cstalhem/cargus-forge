# Outdoor lighting

## Who may do the work
- [standard] Electrical installation work, which includes connecting a product permanently to the electrical installation, may only be done by an authorised electrician or within a registered electrical installation company (elsäkerhetslagen, ELSÄK-FS 2017:2 and 2017:3) [1].
- [standard] Fixed outdoor lighting must, with few exceptions, be installed by a registered electrical installation company [2].
- [standard] A private person may replace a fixed light fitting only in dry indoor spaces in a home; changes to fixed installations and laying cable in the ground are work for an electrical installation company [3].
- [convention] If the user is the least unsure what they may do themselves, they should hire a registered electrical installation company [2][3].

## Products and connection
- [convention] Use only lighting made for outdoor use. It is marked "IP" and two digits (68 is the highest); portable garden products such as light strings should be IP44 or higher, and a product without an IP marking is for indoor use [2].
- [convention] Connect decorative lighting on a balcony or terrace to an earthed outdoor socket, never to an indoor socket, and protect the cables from being crushed [2].
- [convention] Connect all outdoor lighting through a residual current device (jordfelsbrytare). If the outdoor socket has none, use a portable one made for outdoor use, and press its test button at least every six months [2].
- [convention] Extension cords used outdoors should be earthed and have the right IP rating [2].
- [convention] Check that a light string's transformer has the voltage the string needs; a wrong voltage can cause a fire, and a string with no voltage information should not be used [4].
- [convention] Keep halogen lamps, which can reach 250 °C, and LED lamps, which can also get hot, away from combustible material [5].

## Light, neighbours and wildlife
- [convention] Choose low luminaires so less light spreads upwards, and shield and aim them so they do not light up water, woodland edges and other areas that matter to animals and plants [6].
- [convention] Choose warmer (redder) light: it attracts fewer insects than cold white light. Near nature, light should have no ultraviolet component and be amber, at most 2700 K [6][7].
- [convention] Use timers and motion sensors so lights are on only when needed [6][7].
- [convention] Switch lights off above all from April to October, when wildlife is most active, and be sparing in winter too [6].
- [convention] Do not light large trees, especially trees with holes, or water surfaces: it disturbs the trees, pollinating insects, bats and birds [7].
- [taste] A lit entrance and steps only; low bollards or path lights along the walkway; a string of lights over the seating area on a timer; or a dark garden, since darkness is beautiful too [6].

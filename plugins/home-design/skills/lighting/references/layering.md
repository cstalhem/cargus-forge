# Layering light

A room is lit in layers: general light that lights the whole room, task light aimed where you need to see well, and mood light that is soft and diffuse.

## Layers
- [convention] Give every room general light, so you can light the whole room when needed, task light where you need to see well, and mood light; combine all three in every room. [1]
- [convention] Aim for 5 to 10 light sources in a room. [1]
- [convention] In living and dining areas, mix general and task light: one or more ceiling lights for general light, plus wall lights, lights over pictures, uplights for dark corners, and floor or table lamps for tasks. [2]
- [convention] Light from several sources and directions, or indirect light from light-coloured room surfaces, gives soft light and soft shadows. Light from one small, distant source gives hard light and hard shadows. [3]

## Glare
- [convention] Use shades or diffusers so that the lamp itself cannot be seen from any normal viewing direction. [2]
- [convention] Avoid glare by aiming the light right and, where needed, shielding the light from windows and fittings. Sensitivity to glare grows with age. [3]

## Room by room
- [convention] Hall: a ceiling light just inside the front door lights the door and callers. In a larger hall, add a second light further in, and keep the hall about as light as the rooms and stairs that lead off it. [2]
- [convention] Stairs: light the head and foot of the stairs and the landings, and place the light so that treads and risers contrast. A single light at the top lights the treads but leaves the risers in shadow. [2]
- [convention] Kitchen: light the worktops with lights under the wall cabinets, behind a pelmet at the front, which gives light without glare. Plug-in LED strips can run from existing sockets. Keep task lights away from direct heat from the cooker and steam from the kettle. [2]
- [convention] Bedroom: place bedside lamps so that they do not glare when you sit or lie in bed, and consider lights inside wardrobes and over chests of drawers. [2]
- [convention] Bathroom: light the face at the mirror, the shower and the basin. Strip lights high on the walls give even light with few shadows. [2]
- [taste] Living room mood: warm pools of light from table and floor lamps; or indirect light from LED strips on shelves or along the ceiling; or a dimmable ceiling light combined with a few low lamps.
- [taste] Dining table: one pendant hung low over the table; or a row of small pendants over a long table; or candles with a dimmed ceiling light.
- [taste] Windows: a window lamp in each window for the Swedish evening look; or no window lamps, with the room lit from within.

## Control
- [convention] Give the layers their own switches and dimmers, so the light can follow the task and the mood. [2]
- [convention] Make it possible to raise and lower the light level in a room, because the need for light differs between people and grows with age. [3]
- [convention] Presence sensors suit stairwells, halls, basements and entrances. A timer suits a lamp that should come on at set times, such as a window lamp. [4]

# Light sources

How to choose a lamp: how much light it gives in lumen, its colour temperature in Kelvin, how well it renders colour (Ra), and whether it flickers or dims well.

## Brightness in lumen
- [convention] Choose a lamp by how much light it gives, in lumen, not by its watts: watts tell how much power it uses. [1]
- [convention] LED lumen and the old incandescent bulb they replace: 250 lm ≈ 25 W, 400 lm ≈ 40 W, 800 lm ≈ 60 W, 1,000 lm ≈ 75 W, 1,300 lm ≈ 100 W, 2,000 lm ≈ 150 W. An 800 lm LED lamp draws about 7–9 W. [1]
- [convention] For mood light that is always low, choose a lamp of 100–200 lm with a warm colour of about 2,700 K, rather than dimming a brighter lamp. [2]

## Colour temperature in Kelvin
- [convention] The higher the Kelvin value, the cooler and bluer the light: 2,500–2,700 K gives warm light, and 4,000 K or more gives cool light. [3][4]
- [convention] For warm, cosy light, choose about 3,000 K. Keep practical light at no more than about 4,500 K, as cooler light feels clinical. An incandescent bulb is about 2,700 K and a candle about 2,000 K. [5]
- [convention] Daylight is about 5,000 K and renders colours very well. [3]
- [convention] Warm-white LED light brings out yellow and red tones and tones down blue and green, so judge paint and fabric colours under the lamps the room will have. [6]
- [taste] Warm white of about 2,700 K in every room, for an even evening light; or about 3,000 K in the kitchen, bathroom and at the desk, with 2,700 K elsewhere; or tunable smart lamps that move from cooler light by day to warmer light in the evening.

## Colour rendering (Ra)
- [standard] Since 1 September 2021, light sources sold in the EU must have a colour rendering index of at least Ra 80, unless they are high-output discharge lamps or are marked for outdoor, industrial or similar use (EU Regulation 2019/2020). [7]
- [convention] Where colour matters, such as at a make-up mirror, over a kitchen worktop or in a wardrobe, choose Ra 97–99. An incandescent bulb has Ra 99. [5]

## Flicker and dimming
- [standard] Since 1 September 2021, mains LED light sources sold in the EU must keep flicker to Pst LM ≤ 1.0 and the stroboscopic effect to SVM ≤ 0.4 at full load (EU Regulation 2019/2020). [7]
- [convention] A good lamp does not flicker. People differ in how sensitive they are to flicker, and some flicker cannot be seen. [3]
- [convention] Dim an LED lamp only if its package marks it as dimmable, and only on a dimmer made for low LED loads. A trailing-edge dimmer is often gentler on LED lamps. [8]
- [convention] Do not use LED lamps on an old dimmer made only for incandescent bulbs. [5]
- [convention] A lamp and dimmer that suit each other dim without flicker. [2]

# Wet rooms and electrical safety

What a light fitting in a wet room or outdoors must meet, and who may install it in Sweden. The skill uses this file to help the user choose a fitting and hire an electrician. It gives no DIY steps for fixed electrical work.

## Who may do the work
- [standard] Under Swedish electrical safety rules, most electrical installation work must be done by a registered electrical installation company, and changes to fixed installations, such as a new socket or cables in walls, always need one. [1]
- [standard] Under the same rules, a person without authorisation as an electrician may not do electrical installation work privately, or outside a company's self-inspection programme (egenkontrollprogram). [2]
- [standard] A private person with enough knowledge may change bulbs and lamp holders, replace a fixed light fitting in a dry room of a home, fit plugs and cord switches, and replace an existing switch or socket of at most 16 A in its own box. Changes to fixed installations, swapping an unearthed socket for an earthed one, 230 V floor heating and cables in the ground are not allowed. [1]
- [standard] The private person's right to replace a fixed light fitting covers dry rooms only, so a fitting in a bathroom is work for a registered company. [1]
- [standard] By law, every company that does electrical installation work on someone else's installation must be registered with Elsäkerhetsverket, and for work in a home its registration must include the business type Bostäder. A company that is not registered works illegally. [3]
- [standard] The owner of a house or flat is responsible for its electrical installation being safe. [3]
- [convention] Before hiring, check the company in Elsäkerhetsverket's e-service Kolla elföretaget, and ask to see its self-inspection programme. [3]

## IP codes
- [convention] An IP code has two digits: the first, 0 to 6, gives the protection against solid objects, and the second, 0 to 9, the protection against water. An X replaces a digit that is not stated. [4]
- [convention] IP20, the most common class indoors, protects against objects larger than 12 mm and gives no protection against water. IPX4 protects against splashing water from all directions, and IP44 adds protection against objects larger than 1 mm. [4]
- [convention] Outdoors, a fitting always needs protection against water, for example IP23. [4]

## Bathrooms and showers
- [standard] Electrical installations in rooms with a bath or shower follow section 701 of the Swedish wiring rules, SS 436 40 00, which divides the room into zones 0, 1 and 2 by how much water can reach them. [5]
- [standard] Fittings in zone 0 need IPX7. Fittings in zones 1 and 2 need IPX4, and IPX5 where they are exposed to water jets (SS 436 40 00, section 701). [5]
- [standard] In zone 0, only equipment made for that zone may be used, at safety extra-low voltage (SELV) of at most 12 V AC or 30 V DC, and no switches may be placed there (SS 436 40 00, section 701). [5]
- [standard] A light fitting may be installed in zone 1 if it is fixed, permanently connected and made for zone 1 by its maker (SS 436 40 00, section 701). [5]
- [standard] Every circuit that feeds a bath or shower room must be protected by a residual current device (jordfelsbrytare) of at most 30 mA (SS 436 40 00, section 701). [5]
- [convention] The size of each zone and the current wording of the rules are in the current edition of SS 436 40 00; the electrician applies them. [5]

# Light levels

Light level means illuminance: the light that falls on a surface, measured in lux (lumen per square metre). No Swedish standard or regulation sets lux levels for homes. SS-EN 12464-1 sets them for workplaces, so it applies to a desk used for work at home. The levels for the rest of the home are guidance.

## Home office and desk work
- [standard] For normal desk work, such as reading, writing and screen work, light the work area to 500 lx and the room to 300 lx, measured 85 cm above the floor. These are Arbetsmiljöverket's values for normal office work, from SS-EN 12464-1:2021. [1]
- [standard] For work with higher demands on sight, light the work area to 750 lx; for fine drawing work, to 1,500 lx with 500 lx in the room (Arbetsmiljöverket, from SS-EN 12464-1:2021). [1]
- [convention] Place the desk so that no window is behind the screen or mirrored in it, and so that light from lamps and windows does not glare or reflect in the screen. [1]
- [convention] Put the extra light on the work area with a task lamp, rather than raising the light in the whole room, and make both adjustable. [1]
- [convention] Keep light-coloured walls lit to at least about 150 lx, so the room does not feel dark around a bright desk. [1]

## Rooms in the home
- [convention] General light measured on the floor: hall, living and dining room, bathroom and bedroom 100–300 lx; kitchen 200–300 lx; stair treads 100–200 lx. [2]
- [convention] For someone with sight loss and dementia, raise the minimum to 300 lx in the hall, living and dining room and bathroom, 600 lx in the kitchen, 200 lx in the bedroom and 150 lx on stair treads. [2]
- [convention] Keep the light level in a room no more than twice the level in the room next to it, so the eyes need not adapt sharply when you move between them. [2]

## Tasks
- [convention] Light on the task, from the minimum for someone with sight loss up to the level for someone with sight loss and dementia: reading and writing 200–1,000 lx; cooking and making tea 200–1,000 lx; shaving 200–1,000 lx; washing up and meals 200–500 lx; using the phone 100–400 lx; brushing teeth 200–300 lx; showering and putting on shoes 100–300 lx; choosing clothes 100–200 lx. [2]
- [convention] A person with sight loss may need up to 1,000 lx for some tasks at home. [2]

## Age and daylight
- [convention] Older people need more light, and their eyes become more sensitive to glare. [3]
- [convention] Make it possible to raise and lower the light level in a room, because the need for light differs between people and grows with age. Avoid large differences in brightness, because adapting too often tires the eyes. [4]
- [convention] Use the daylight first. Rooms with light walls, ceilings and floors need fewer lamps than dark rooms, because light surfaces reflect light and dark ones absorb it. [3]
- [convention] By day, place a reading chair so that daylight falls over the shoulder of someone sitting with their back to the window. [2]

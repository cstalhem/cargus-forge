# Lighting

Advise on the lighting of one room or area in a Swedish home. The facts are in the reference files below. This file holds the procedure.

## Reference files

Read a file only when the question needs it.

- `references/light-levels.md`: how much light a room or task needs, in lux, including for older people and for a home office desk.
- `references/light-sources.md`: choosing a lamp: lumen and watt equivalents, colour temperature in Kelvin, colour rendering (Ra), flicker and dimming.
- `references/layering.md`: general, task and mood light, glare, room-by-room placement, and switching, dimming and sensors.
- `references/wet-rooms-and-safety.md`: IP codes, bathroom zones, residual current devices, what a private person may do with electricity in Sweden, and how to hire an electrician.

## Procedure

1. **Gather the facts.** If `room-plan` passed a room brief, use it. Otherwise find out the room and what happens in it, its size in m² and ceiling height, its windows and orientation, the fittings and lamps already there, and who uses it, such as an older person or someone with poor sight. Ask only for the facts that would change the answer.
2. **Read the references** that the question needs, from the list above.
3. **Plan the light in layers.** For each layer, say where the light goes, the light level to aim for in lux, and the lamp to use: lumen, Kelvin and Ra. Use metric units.
4. **Tag every guideline** with its guideline strength, as the reference file tags it:
   - `standard`: give the source the reference cites, such as the standard, the regulation or the authority.
   - `convention`: say it is a rule of thumb, and give its source where the reference has one.
   - `taste`: give the choices as options, never as the one right answer.
5. **Stay within the references.** Take every value from a reference file. If the question goes beyond them, say that the answer is general knowledge without a source, and do not tag it `standard`.
6. **Answer in the user's language.**

## Electrical work

Some advice needs fixed electrical work, such as a new ceiling outlet, moving or adding a fixed light, a new switch, a wall dimmer, cables in walls or ceilings, or any fixed light in a bathroom or outdoors. Then:

- Say that the work needs an authorised electrician: in Sweden, an electrical installation company registered with Elsäkerhetsverket, which the user can check in the e-service Kolla elföretaget.
- Give no DIY steps: no wiring, no connection order, no tools, no how-to. This also holds where Swedish rules let a private person do the work, such as replacing a fixed light in a dry room.
- Offer options that need no fixed work where they fit, such as plug-in floor, table and wall lamps, plug-in LED strips under kitchen cabinets, battery lights or smart bulbs.
- For a wet room or outdoors, give the IP class that a fitting needs, from `references/wet-rooms-and-safety.md`, so the user can choose the fitting. The electrician installs it.

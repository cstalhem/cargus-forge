# Textiles and art

## Curtains

- [convention] Mount the curtain rod about 10 to 15 cm above the window frame. If the top of the frame is 15 to 30 cm below the ceiling, mount it halfway between frame and ceiling. Go no higher than about 20 cm above the frame, unless the window reaches the ceiling, where the rod goes at the ceiling line [1].
- [convention] Let the rod reach about 8 to 15 cm past the frame on each side, and make it no more than a third longer than the window is wide. Small kitchen and bathroom windows often look best with the rod inside the frame [1].
- [convention] Make the total curtain width, all panels together, 2 to 2.5 times the window's width [1].
- [convention] End curtains that are opened every day about 1 cm above the floor. Curtains that stay in place can break on the floor with about 8 cm extra, or pool with 10 to 15 cm extra, though pooled fabric gathers dust and pet hair [1].
- [convention] If a radiator, a sofa or a deep sill sits under the window, end the curtains just above it [1].
- [taste] Sheer curtains that filter daylight; lined curtains that dim the room; blackout curtains for bedrooms on light Nordic summer nights; a sheer layer with a blackout layer behind it.

## Blinds and cords

- [convention] Fasten the cords of blinds, roller blinds and awnings so that a small child cannot reach them or become entangled, as Konsumentverket advises; hanging cords carry a strangulation risk [2].
- [standard] Cord safety devices for internal blinds are specified in SS-EN 16434:2014, which also covers devices that you can fit to existing blinds; Konsumentverket says older installations should be checked and fixed too [3].

## Upholstery, cushions and throws

- [standard] A fabric's abrasion resistance is given in Martindale rubs, measured by the method in EN ISO 12947-2:2016 [4].
- [convention] For a sofa in a home, choose upholstery fabric rated above 25,000 Martindale; fabrics for home use range from about 12,000 to 40,000 [5].
- [convention] Light fastness is graded 1 to 8, and 5 to 6 suits indoor use; every fabric fades in sunlight, so keep upholstered furniture out of direct sun [5].
- [convention] Keep upholstered furniture at least 20 cm from a radiator or other heat source, and further from an open fireplace or stove [5].
- [convention] Velvet, chenille and plush show pressure marks and shift in shade with the light; this is normal for pile fabrics [5].
- [taste] Cushions and throws in tones of the room's main colour, varied by texture (linen, wool, bouclé, velvet); cushions in one accent colour; mixed patterns tied together by a shared colour.

## Chemicals in textiles

- [convention] Wash new textiles before you use them, to keep excess chemicals from manufacturing off the skin, or buy second-hand textiles instead, as Kemikalieinspektionen advises [6].
- [convention] Kemikalieinspektionen points to the ecolabels Svanen, EU Ecolabel, Bra Miljöval and GOTS for textiles, because they set requirements on the whole production chain [6].
- [convention] Older wool blankets and rugs may have been treated against moths and other pests with DDT; Kemikalieinspektionen says to take them to the municipal recycling centre as combustible waste, not to textile collection [6].

## Rugs and fall safety

- [convention] Remove loose rugs, or lay anti-slip underlay under them, and keep cords off the floor, as Socialstyrelsen advises to prevent falls; most fall accidents happen at home [7].
- [convention] For rug sizes in relation to furniture, use the `furniture-layout` skill.

## Candles near textiles

- [convention] Keep lit candles away from curtains and other textiles, use holders of a material that cannot burn (wrought iron, ceramic, stone), never set many tealights close together on one tray, and never leave lit candles unattended, as Brandskyddsföreningen advises [8].

## Art

- [convention] Keep an art arrangement no wider than the furniture below it; above a sofa, about two thirds of the sofa's width [9].
- [convention] Start the art about 20 to 25 cm above the top of the furniture, and centre it near average eye level, about 145 to 152 cm above the floor; hang lower in rooms where people mostly sit. The `furniture-layout` skill holds the full height rules [9].
- [convention] In a grid of frames, leave about 8 to 10 cm between them; in a triptych, about 10 to 15 cm; for works of the same size, keep the gaps equal on all sides [9].
- [convention] Plan an arrangement with paper templates taped to the wall, or lay it out on the floor, before you drill [9].
- [taste] A symmetric grid of identical frames and same-size works; a salon-style wall of mixed frames and sizes, held together by a shared colour, mount or frame [9].

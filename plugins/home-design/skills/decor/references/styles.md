# Styles

## Choosing a style

- [convention] Start from what the home already has, such as the building's era, the floor and the pieces the user keeps; a style that fits them needs fewer changes than one that fights them.
- [taste] One leading style throughout; one leading style with a second as accent (for example a Scandinavian room with a few mid-century pieces); an established blend such as Japandi.
- [taste] A style kept close to its source (a full Gustavian room); a style used loosely, as a palette and a few signature pieces in an otherwise plain room.

## Nordic and Swedish styles

- [taste] Scandinavian: neutral colours with tonal, textured pieces; simple light-wood furniture layered with soft textiles; natural wood, with more industrial materials for lights and shelving [1]. Swedish design puts function and simple, clean lines first [2].
- [taste] Gustavian (late 1700s, from the reign of Gustav III): whites, pale blues, greens and soft greys; furniture painted in soft pastels, often in local pine; upright chair backs and tapering legs; laurel wreaths, festoons and other classical motifs; symmetry and restrained decoration [3].
- [taste] Larsson style, after Carl and Karin Larsson's home in Sundborn: a colourful interior built around Karin Larsson's textile art and handicraft [4].
- [taste] Funkis (Swedish functionalism, which broke through at the 1930 Stockholm Exhibition): furniture designed from its function first, simple and easy to clean, with concrete, stainless steel and masonite as the main materials [7].

## Other common styles

- [taste] Japandi, a blend of Japanese and Scandinavian: calm neutrals (beige, grey, taupe, cream) with black accents; wood and clay, linen and matte finishes; low-clutter rooms with fewer, functional, tactile objects [1].
- [taste] Mid-century modern (about 1940 to 1970): tapered legs, clean silhouettes and geometric forms in warm wood, with a few sculptural pieces and vivid colour in art, paint and textiles [1].
- [taste] Industrial: exposed brick, concrete, steel and wood with their textures left visible, softened by upholstered seating, rugs and warm lighting [1].
- [taste] Minimalist: neutral palette and white walls, clean lines, good storage and few decorative objects [1].
- [taste] Bohemian: contrasting patterns and colours drawn from nature; vintage and artisan-made pieces; layered rugs, cushions, throws and plants instead of matched sets [1].
- [taste] Farmhouse: white and cream walls and textiles; wood furniture and floorboards, including distressed finishes; sturdy pieces and neutral rugs [1].
- [taste] Rustic: exposed wood or brick, stone, aged wood and wrought iron, natural fabrics and warm lighting [1].
- [taste] Traditional: warm tones and dark wood finishes, classic furniture lines and layered, cosy fabrics [1].
- [taste] Coastal: light blues, creams and whites; whitewashed wood, driftwood, glass, linen and cane; light, airy furniture [1].
- [taste] Art deco: bold jewel tones; marble, mirrored glass, lacquer, brass and velvet; geometric forms with ornate decoration [1].

## Styling objects

- [convention] Group decorative objects in odd numbers, most often three, and vary their height, form and proportions within the group [5].
- [convention] Mix textures within a group or a room, such as wood, ceramic, linen and metal, so that a room in few colours still has depth [5].
- [convention] Use pairs instead where symmetry or use asks for them, such as matched lamps, bedside tables or end tables [6].
- [taste] Symmetric arrangements, which read as formal and calm; asymmetric arrangements, which read as relaxed and collected.
- [taste] Few, larger objects with empty surface around them; many smaller objects gathered on trays or in one cabinet.
